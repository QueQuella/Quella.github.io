How game space influence players' immersion and perceptions of real-world space: a study of Exit 8 style games

View The Exit 8 on Steam ↗
01 / Abstract

Abstract

A subway corridor or a hotel hallway is usually a space we pass through without much attention. In games such as The Exit 8, Next Business Trip and Room 13, familiar transitional spaces become environments of repetition, observation and uncertainty. Players move through apparently ordinary settings, identify subtle anomalies and reconsider what they expect to see. This proposal asks how the overlap between virtual and everyday spaces may shape immersion and enjoyment, and whether that experience may influence how players perceive similar places outside the game.

The study brings together spatial presence and flow, Goffman’s frame analysis, and Foucault’s concept of heterotopia. These perspectives provide a way to consider how players feel located within a game space, become absorbed in its actions and reinterpret familiar settings through the frame of play. Rather than treating the virtual environment as a background, the proposal considers space as an active part of the experience: recognition establishes familiarity, while unexpected differences invite closer attention.

Three hypotheses connect spatial overlap with immersion and enjoyment, and then with possible changes in real-world spatial perception and attitudes toward everyday habits. The proposal develops a theoretical model for further investigation. Its central interest is whether an experience designed around noticing small changes can extend beyond the screen, encouraging players to look at ordinary places differently.

02 / Research Purpose

Research Purpose

Recently, games, like Exit 8, Next Business Trip and Room 13, have emerged as a distinctive genre and go viral on media platforms. Those games, Exit 8 style games (E8 games), can be referred as first person psychological horror game, with simple mechanics of finding the differences and walking simulation in repeated and familiar spaces. However, why the E8 game spaces overlaps with the real-world spaces? According to Foucault’s Heterotopias as Play Spaces, E8 games bring multiple impossible spaces together into a single space and create heterotopia spaces that can let players reflect on the society, leading heated discussion online and even attitude change in the boring daily routine (Hutchings & Giardino, 2016). For instance, after audience watching bloggers' live-streaming records, when they pass through the subway corridor, they may pay more attention on the surroundings and think of interesting things that happen in games. To figure out how the game spaces make players feel immersive and reshape their perception of real-world spaces, the study seeks to discover the roles of game space in players’ enjoyment and perception of the real-world spaces. The investigation builds on Goffman's frame analysis, the concept of heterotopia, as well as presence and flow theory.

03 / Literature Review

Literature Review

Presence and flow theory can be defined as the sensation of being there and getting involved in game, which further influences enjoyment, performance and popularity of the game (Weibel & Wissmath, 2011). In game world, we feel spacial presence and a perceptual illusion of non-mediation, rather than physical presence. In terms of frame analysis, when players spot the notice of game rules or are shocked by the unusual accidents, they will jump out of the frame of real-world space and adopt the game world as their primary frame (Zerubavel, 2024). After players entering a state of flow, their attention is narrowly focused on in‑game actions and in turn, influencing enjoyment and performance.

The flow theory and spatial presence have been developed to investigate VR shopping experiences and other immersive experiences in digital environments, particularly in understanding how immersive technologies create meaningful psychological engagement. First, Weibel and Wissmath (2011) separately examines presence and flow and concludes that, although conceptually related, they are empirically distinct states but closely related. Another study on virtual education environments shows that, presence and flow shape learners’ intention to continue e-learning (Rodríguez-Ardura & Meseguer-Artola, 2021). More research focus on how to explain users’ engagement with narrative media in a complex presence and flow model (Pianzola et al., 2021). Therefore, the theory has been extensively applied to explain gameplay engagement.

However, few studies have applied the theory to explain how to affect game players' attitude and further influence behavior changes. It could be argued that the presence and flow theory can have further extension towards players' performance. In the E8 games, players feel the system is easy and useful, having positive attitude and strong intention to continue playing. Then, as Bateson's essay "A Theory of Play and Fantasy" indicates, those transitional spaces, like subway and business hotel, once are considered as play spaces, they will partially lose their original meanings and are not merely performative and functional (Bateson, 1955). From immersing and rethinking the game space to breaking the link towards reality, E8 games try to let players observe the details in common but paranormal public spaces. Just like the behavior intention in presence and flow theory, players may reflect the game space as a heterotopia on the real-world space and continue observing the abnormal in their daily routine.

Therefore, I predict that:

H1: The overlap between the game world and the real world has positive effect on players' immersion and enjoyment in games.

H2: The more immersion and enjoyment players gain, the more likely they may change their perceptions of the real-world spaces.

H3: The more immersion and enjoyment players gain, the more positive attitude they have towards changing their habits.
